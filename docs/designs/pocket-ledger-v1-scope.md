## Problem Statement

Abdullah and his friends are trying to budget and save a target amount for a trip next summer. None of them has a system: no spreadsheet, no notes app, not even a habit of checking the bank app. Pocket Ledger is the tool meant to close that gap — log a purchase in under 10 seconds, see the month at a glance, import bank CSVs instead of typing history by hand.

It is also the portfolio piece for data science internship applications, which means the model layer has to be real and measured, not decorative.

## Status Quo

Nothing systematic. No spreadsheet, no notes file, no app, no regular balance check.

This matters more than it looks. Free, pre-installed alternatives already exist — his bank's app, Mint, YNAB — and he uses none of them. For him today, the problem has not crossed the threshold where a person takes action. The competitor is not YNAB. It is doing nothing, and doing nothing is currently winning.

Cost of the workaround: he does not know whether he is on pace for the trip. That is the pain the product gets paid in.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**User zero:** Abdullah. Student in Canada, saving toward a specific summer trip with friends.

**Second user:** an unnamed friend from the trip group. Naming them is part of the assignment.

**Narrowest wedge:** an installed app where logging a purchase takes under 10 seconds and the month view answers "how am I doing" without any setup beyond signing up. No CSV import, no model, no insights. If that does not get opened in week three, nothing downstream matters.

## Premises

1. Pocket Ledger is a monthly spending tracker, as CLAUDE.md section 1 describes. The trip-savings framing is recorded as a finding and deferred, not built into v1. — agreed
2. Abdullah is user zero. The first real test is four consecutive weeks of his own entries; friends come after. — agreed
3. The retention gap is real and the roadmap addresses it nowhere: there is no reminder, notification, streak or weekly nudge in any of the 11 phases. v1 ships without one and accepts the risk. — agreed
4. CSV import belongs before the model layer, because the models need real data to train and evaluate on. — agreed
5. The 11-phase roadmap is longer than the demand evidence justifies, so v1 ends at Phase 4. — agreed (see Recommended Approach)

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Daily driver first (Phases 0-4) — CHOSEN
Summary: build auth, quick add, transaction list, monthly view and budgets, then EAS-build an APK and use it for four weeks before starting the model layer.
Effort: M (human: ~3 weeks / CC: ~6-8 sessions). Risk: Low.
Pros: tests the riskiest assumption (will he open it) while changing course is still cheap; every later phase gets real transactions instead of only synthetic rows; the repo shows a working app early.
Cons: no ML in the repo for the first few weeks; adds an EAS build step the roadmap only planned once at Phase 11.
Reuses: the roadmap's Phase 0-4 ordering unchanged, so CLAUDE.md section 7 needs no rewrite.

### Approach B: Roadmap as written (all 11 phases)
Rejected during the session. Finding out whether the app gets used in month three, after five ML sub-projects are built, inverts the risk order. Highest chance of a half-finished repo, which reads worse to an interviewer than a small complete one.

### Approach C: DS-forward reorder
Rejected during the session. Gets a trained model into the repo fastest, but leaves the app unusable day to day for longer, so Abdullah never becomes user zero, and the phase numbering stops matching CLAUDE.md.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach A.** Ship Phases 0 through 4 to a real phone, then dogfood for four weeks before Phase 5.

Rationale: demand is unproven and the founder tracks nothing today, so the cheapest information available is whether he uses his own app. Everything after Phase 4 improves with real data — the classifier trains on his actual merchants, the forecast backtests on his actual months, the anomaly rules see his actual outliers. Building the model layer first means training on synthetic data and hoping.

Recorded as decision `676625b9-36aa-4915-9ce6-2d2b0d0a36e1`. Ceiling: no ML in the repo for the first few weeks. Upgrade trigger: 28 days of real self-logged entries exist, or an internship deadline requires the model reports sooner than April.

## Success Criteria

1. APK installed on Abdullah's own phone at the end of Phase 4, not Phase 11.
2. Quick add measured at under 10 seconds, stopwatch on five real entries, recorded.
3. 28 consecutive days with at least one logged transaction on every day he spent money. Count it honestly; a gap is a finding.
4. At least one other person from the trip group has it installed and has logged 10 or more transactions.
5. `npm test`, `npm run typecheck` and `npm run lint` clean on every commit.

## The Assignment

**For the next seven days, log every purchase in your phone's notes app. One line: amount, where, what for.**

Not in Pocket Ledger. It does not exist yet. That is the point.

This costs about ten seconds per purchase and it buys two things no amount of code can:

1. **It tests the only assumption the whole project rests on** — that you will actually do this. If you miss three days with a notes app, a prettier app will not save you, and that is worth knowing in week one rather than month three.
2. **It produces the first real fixture data.** Seven days of your own messy merchant strings is what Phase 5's parser tests and Phase 6's normalizer get measured against. Real strings, not invented ones.

Second half, same week: text the trip group and ask what each of them uses to track money right now. You need one name for the Open Questions list, and their answer tells you which banks' CSV formats actually matter.
